## Overview

Building on the mechanistic ECO-Light experiment at Hebei University, we scaled up to two field transects that use **the same light treatment and the same insect traps**: one spanning China's main natural grassland types and one spanning the real light-pollution gradient between cities and countryside in the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei region. Data from all three platforms can be combined directly.

## Network 1: Grassland transect

Night is an active time in grassland ecosystems. If dark nights are lit by artificial light, how do insect communities and respiratory carbon release change — and does the same lamp have the same effect in different grasslands? From the Inner Mongolian steppe to the Tibetan Plateau, grasslands differ widely in climate, vegetation and biota, which may set their sensitivity to light at night.

- Light experiments at eight existing transect sites — Erguna, Xieertala, Xilinhot, Youyu, Urat, Hongyuan, Naqu and Baingoin — covering meadow steppe, typical steppe, desert steppe, alpine meadow and alpine steppe.
- Each site has a control and an ALAN treatment with five replicates each (10 plots), with a common target illuminance of 30 lx.
- Respiration and aboveground insect communities (composition, diversity, abundance and biomass) are measured in the same plots.

**Research questions**

1. How consistent are the ecological effects of light at night across natural grasslands, and which grassland types respond differently?
2. Which climatic and vegetation conditions make a grassland more sensitive to light at night?
3. Are changes in insect communities linked to changes in respiratory carbon release, and does this link depend on the environment?

## Network 2: Urban–rural transect (ECO-Trait)

Night-time lighting can filter insect functional traits such as body size, which govern dispersal, energy use and reproduction. How background light levels along urban–rural gradients shape these traits needs coordinated multi-site observation.

- Twelve sites across Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei, six urban and six rural (Table 1).
- The same flight-interception and pitfall traps as ECO-Light, with synchronised weekly sampling and standardised protocols at all sites.
- Insects are identified in the lab and measured for body length, body width and wing length to analyse how light pollution affects communities and individual traits.

## How the three platforms fit together

- **ECO-Light** (Hebei University): a controlled single-site experiment that reveals mechanisms.
- **Grassland transect**: a cross-regional experiment that tests how general the effects are and under which conditions they hold.
- **Urban–rural transect**: a survey of insect traits along real light-pollution gradients.

With identical sampling equipment, the three can be analysed together to understand how artificial light at night affects insect communities, species interactions and grassland carbon cycling.
